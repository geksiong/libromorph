# Classical Philosophy & Vertical Layouts

[T]{.dropcap}hroughout East Asian history, literary works and philosophical treatises were traditionally copied and read in vertical columns progressing from right to left (縱書 / 縦書き). In this chapter, we showcase LibroMorph's vertical layout system, combined with classical [Daoist philosophy]{.index entry="Philosophy!Daoism"} and poetry.

## The Dao De Jing (道德經)

Attributed to [Laozi]{.index entry="Philosophy!Laozi"} (老子, {Lǎozǐ|ㄌㄠˇ ㄗˇ}), the opening chapter of the *Dao De Jing* contemplates the ineffable nature of ultimate reality:

> {道|dào}可{道|dào}，{非|fēi}{常|cháng}{道|dào}。  
> {名|míng}可{名|míng}，{非|fēi}{常|cháng}{名|míng}。  
> {無|wú}{名|míng}{天|tiān}{地|dì}{之|zhī}{始|shǐ}；  
> {有|yǒu}{名|míng}{萬|wàn}{物|wù}{之|zhī}{母|mǔ}。

*English Translation:*
> The Way that can be spoken of is not the constant Way;  
> The name that can be named is not the constant Name.  
> The Nameless is the origin of Heaven and Earth;  
> The Named is the mother of myriad things.

## The Butterfly Dream of Zhuangzi (莊子)

The famous parable of [Zhuangzi]{.index entry="Philosophy!Zhuangzi"} (莊周) questioning the boundary between dreaming and waking:

> 昔者{莊周|Zhuāng Zhōu}夢為{胡蝶|húdié}，{栩栩然|xǔxǔrán}{胡蝶|húdié}也，自{喻|yù}適志與！不知{周|Zhōu}也。  
> 俄然{覺|jué}，則{蘧蘧然|qúqúrán}{周|Zhōu}也。不知{周|Zhōu}之夢為{胡蝶|húdié}與，{胡蝶|húdié}之夢為{周|Zhōu}與？

## Li Bai's Drinking Alone Under the Moon

The Tang dynasty poet [Li Bai]{.index entry="Literature!Li Bai"} (李白) writing on solitude and cosmic companionship:

> {花間|huā jiān}一壺酒，獨酌無相親。  
> 舉杯邀{明月|míng yuè}，對影成三人。  
> 月既不解飲，影徒隨我身。  
> 暫伴月將影，行樂須及春。

## Philosophical Reflection Quiz

::: quiz
**Question 3:** In the opening line of Laozi's *Dao De Jing*, what does the phrase 「常道」 (*cháng dào*) represent?

- [ ] A temporary political decree
- [ ] A rigid written legal code
- [x] The eternal, unchanging Way
- [ ] The military arts

*Explanation:* 「常」 (*cháng*) denotes constancy, permanence, and eternity. 「常道」 is the enduring, primordial Dao beyond transient human language.
